How we pick tools

We only list tools that are publicly available, actively maintained, and genuinely used by real people — no vapourware and no paid placements. Each entry is checked for what it actually does, what it costs to start, and who it suits best. Pricing labels are simple on purpose: Free means usable at no cost, Freemium means a free tier with paid upgrades, and Paid means you will need a subscription to do anything meaningful. Tools change fast, so we revisit listings regularly and drop anything that shuts down or stops delivering.

Frequently asked questions

Are the tools on this page free?

Some are, some are not — that is exactly why every card carries a pricing label. Freemium tools let you start free and charge for heavier use or better models; Paid tools need a subscription. Check the label before you sign up, and read our free vs paid guide for the full picture.

How is this directory different from just searching?

Search results are full of listicles written for clicks, affiliate rankings, and tools that no longer exist. This directory is deliberately small: every tool here is live, well-known enough to have a track record, and described in our own words rather than copied marketing copy.

Which AI tool should a complete beginner start with?

Start with one general assistant — ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini — and use it daily for a week on real tasks: emails, summaries, plans, questions. That teaches you more about what AI can and cannot do than any review, and it makes every specialised tool easier to judge afterwards.

Do these tools keep my data private?

It varies, and it matters. Consumer chatbots may use your conversations to improve their models unless you opt out or pay for a business tier. Never paste passwords, financial records, or confidential work documents into a free tool without checking its data policy first.

Can I suggest a tool for the directory?

The directory is curated rather than exhaustive, and we add tools when they have proven themselves with real users over time. If a tool you love is missing, it usually means we are still watching it — not that we have overlooked it.
